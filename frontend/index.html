A request is not a message. It is a record.

In a chat thread, a complaint is a sentence someone has to remember. In EstateOps it is an object with a location, a priority, an owner, a vendor, a cost and a history — and it cannot be closed without evidence.

Five states, and no way to skip one.

The workflow is enforced by the platform, not by someone remembering to follow up. Each stage names who acts and what the stage produces.

Reported

A resident or owner logs the issue against their flat, with photos and an urgency.

ProducesA request, located and timestamped

Triaged

The manager sets category and priority, and confirms the location is correct.

ProducesA category, a priority, an owner

Assigned

A vendor is matched by trade and issued a work order with a scheduled window.

ProducesA work order and a quotation

Repaired

The technician updates progress on site and uploads completion evidence.

ProducesEvidence and an invoice

Verified

The resident confirms the fix; the manager approves the cost and closes the record.

ProducesA closed request and a cost entry

Four roles. One rule: you see your own scope.

Access is not a setting someone has to remember to tick. It is derived from the role and the unit a person is attached to, and it is applied on every request the server answers.

Nothing closes without evidence

A work order cannot be marked complete without completion evidence, and a request cannot close until the person who reported it has confirmed the fix.

Spend has a ceiling before it has an invoice

Quotations go through an approval limit set per property. Anything above it waits for a named approver rather than arriving as a surprise invoice.

The history cannot be quietly rewritten

Every state change is appended with the person and the time. Correcting a record adds an entry — it does not erase the one before it.
